# Teaching & Pedagogy

My teaching philosophy is built on active learning, inclusive mentorship, and bridging theoretical concepts with hands-on computational tools. I aim to cultivate critical inquiry and technical independence in every classroom.

## Philosophy & Approach

- **Student-Centered Learning:** Emphasizing problem-based learning where students engage directly with primary literature and real-world datasets.
- **Inclusive Classroom Environment:** Creating accessible learning pathways for students from diverse technical backgrounds.
- **Tool-Driven Pedagogy:** Integrating modern computational workflows (Git, Python, interactive notebooks) early in the curriculum.

### North Carolina State University
- **CSC 595 Cybersecurity Practicum** *(Fall 2025)*
Application of cybersecurity principles and computer science and engineering to solve a cybersecurity problem. A project with comprehensive written and oral project report is required.
- **CSC 216 Software Development Fundamentals** *(Fall 2025)*
The second course in computing, intended for majors and minors. Emphasis is on exploring encapsulation; polymorphism; composition; inheritance; finite state machines; linear data structures [including array lists, linked lists, stacks, queues, and the tradeoffs in implementation]; and recursion [including recursive linked lists] in the context of the software development lifecycle including the processes and practices for designing, implementing, and testing high quality software.
- **CSC 472 Cybersecurity Projects** *(Spring 2025)*
Application of cybersecurity principles and basic computer science to solve a cybersecurity problem.
- **CSC 379 Ethics in Computing** *(Fall 2024, Spring 2025)*
This course examines the role of the computing professional in modern society, focusing on the ethical and moral responsibilities of the profession. Additional topics will include moral/ethical decision-making, whistle-blowing, global organizations, licensure, and emerging technologies.
- **CSC 298 Introduction to Computer Science Research Methods** *(Fall 2023)*  
Introduction to computer science research methods for first or second year Computer Science undergraduate students. Topics include creating research problems, reading research papers, conducting a literature review, designing research studies, qualitative and quantitative data analysis, communication of research findings, teamwork, and research directions. Students will work on teams to complete a mentored research proposal.
